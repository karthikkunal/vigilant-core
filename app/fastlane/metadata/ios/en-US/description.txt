vigilant-core tells you what a domain or subdomain really exposes, and reminds you
before something you rely on expires. It is built for people who maintain a
personal site, a side project, or a homelab service, and for operators who need a
lightweight way to keep an eye on a small set of sites.

There is no vigilant-core account and no vigilant-core backend. Everything runs from
your device.

ENTER A DOMAIN AND SEE WHAT IT HIDES

* Registration expiry, registrar, status codes and nameservers, via RDAP
* The TLS certificate leaf AND the full chain the server actually presents
* DNS records — A, AAAA, MX, NS, TXT, CAA
* Email authentication — SPF, DMARC and DKIM
* Certificate Transparency entries, plus passively discovered subdomains
* HTTP status, redirect chain and HSTS

vigilant-core reads the certificate chain from the live TLS handshake rather than
from a high-level HTTP client, so it can tell you when a server is serving a leaf
certificate whose intermediate is missing or expired — a real cause of
intermittent failures that most checkers miss.

EXPIRY REMINDERS, SCHEDULED ON YOUR DEVICE

When vigilant-core finds a registration or certificate expiry, it schedules local
notifications 30, 14, 7 and 1 day ahead. Those reminders are scheduled on your
iPhone, so they arrive even if you never open the app again. Nothing is scheduled
on a server, because there is no server.

WATCH WHAT YOU DEPEND ON

Add a domain, a TCP host and port, or an HTTP endpoint with a body assertion, and
vigilant-core keeps the last check, its provider, and whether the result is stale
visible on the monitor's detail screen — so "is this actually being checked?" is
always answerable at a glance. On iOS, discovery and checks run while the app is
open; continuous background monitoring that pages you the moment something breaks
is an Android feature in this release.

PRIVACY AND NETWORK USE

vigilant-core itself does not store your data on a server. A scan is not offline,
though: the domain you choose is sent to the public lookup services that answer
the question — RDAP, DNS-over-HTTPS, and Certificate Transparency. Those services
see the domain you queried, and some publish their query logs. Review their
policies before scanning a domain whose existence you need to keep private.

No account. No analytics. No advertising. No tracking SDKs. Monitors, reports and
settings stay in private storage on your device, and deleting the app deletes
them.
